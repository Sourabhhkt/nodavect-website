Planning challenge

Infrastructure decisions are interconnected.

Pipeline additions, facility capacities, disposal access, treatment options, storage, and routing decisions affect one another. Nodavect is designed to evaluate these tradeoffs together rather than one asset at a time.

01

Infrastructure selection

Compare candidate pipelines, capacities, connections, and facility investments against system-wide operating implications.

02

Flow & routing strategy

Evaluate how produced water can move through existing and candidate infrastructure while respecting capacities and operating constraints.

03

Scenario comparison

Test alternative forecasts, cost assumptions, infrastructure options, and operating conditions before committing to a plan.

Approach

Optimization as a decision-support layer.

The goal is not to replace planners. It is to give them a rigorous way to test more combinations, quantify tradeoffs, and compare a recommended plan with the current planning baseline.

1. Encode the planning case Represent the network, alternatives, costs, capacities, and operating constraints.
2. Evaluate feasible alternatives Use optimization to search across infrastructure and routing decisions jointly.
3. Compare against the baseline Summarize recommended investments, operating flows, and cost differences in planner-friendly outputs.
Pilot projects

Pressure-test the model on a real planning case.

Nodavect is seeking focused pilot opportunities where an operator or infrastructure planner has a network expansion, routing, or capacity decision that can be evaluated against an existing plan or historical case.

A pilot can help answer questions such as:

  • Which candidate connections or pipelines are worth building?
  • Where could additional treatment, storage, or disposal capacity create value?
  • How should water be routed across the network under projected volumes?
  • How sensitive is the recommended plan to assumptions or forecast changes?

Typical output

  • Recommended infrastructure decisions
  • Illustrative flow allocation across the network
  • Capital and operating cost comparison
  • Scenario and sensitivity discussion
  • Clear comparison with the customer's current planning baseline

Trust & confidentiality

Start with a bounded case and clear data expectations.

Early pilot work should be structured so that the customer knows what information is needed, what will be produced, and how project data will be handled.

Confidential customer data

Project-specific confidentiality and data-handling terms can be established before any sensitive information is transferred.

Focused scope

Pilots can begin with a bounded planning case, including sanitized or historical data where appropriate, before expanding to a broader deployment.

Transparent assumptions

Model inputs, planning assumptions, infrastructure alternatives, and cost drivers are made explicit so recommendations can be reviewed by the planning team.

Decision support, not a black box

Outputs are intended to support engineering and planning judgment with a quantitative comparison of alternatives, rather than replace it.
